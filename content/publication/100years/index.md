abstract: "US business cycles since 1900, examined through a multivariate trend-cycle model consistent with a New Keynesian economy, reveal robust reduced-form regularities between slack, prices and unemployment. Once long-term trends are accounted for, a single common cycle underlies output, unemployment, prices, inflation expectations and the policy rate, and behaves as a demand cycle. The slope of inflation on unemployment is −1.08 over the century, against −0.40 in the raw data, and around −0.80 in postwar subsamples. Okun's law, connecting unemployment and output, is an equally robust relation in the data."
